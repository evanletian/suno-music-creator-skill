# Genre Library

Treat a genre as a coordinated set of production choices, not a label to stack indiscriminately. Start with one lead genre, then add only compatible traits that serve the lyric and emotional target.

## Pop

- **Tempo tendency:** Usually moderate to upbeat, with a steady pulse that supports a concise hook.
- **Groove:** Clean backbeat, four-on-the-floor, or syncopated programmed percussion with an immediate downbeat.
- **Core instruments:** Punchy drums, bass, synths, piano or guitar accents, and hook-defining ear candy.
- **Vocal delivery:** Clear, direct, melody-first lead with layered doubles or harmonies in the chorus.
- **Arrangement movement:** Verses leave space; pre-choruses build tension; choruses widen through layers, register, or rhythm.
- **Mix character:** Polished, vocal-forward, bright enough for hook clarity, with controlled low end.
- **Compatible fusions:** R&B/soul, electronic, folk, hip-hop, cinematic accents.
- **Likely conflicts:** Dense jazz harmony, unrestrained metal distortion, or lo-fi degradation can blur a pop hook unless kept as accents.

## Rock

- **Tempo tendency:** Moderate to fast, from a driving mid-tempo pulse to urgent uptempo momentum.
- **Groove:** Live-feeling kick-snare backbeat, often pushed by eighth-note guitars or tom movement.
- **Core instruments:** Electric guitar riffs, bass guitar, acoustic or electric drum kit, and occasional organ or piano.
- **Vocal delivery:** Assertive, human, and dynamic; can move from intimate verses to a projected chorus.
- **Arrangement movement:** Riff introduction, band entrances, guitar/drum escalation, and a contrast section before the final return.
- **Mix character:** Physical drums, present guitars, punchy midrange, and enough transient detail to retain band energy.
- **Compatible fusions:** Folk, pop, metal, cinematic, electronic texture.
- **Likely conflicts:** Smooth R&B restraint, fragile lo-fi ambience, or intricate jazz harmony can compete with riff-driven force.

## Folk

- **Tempo tendency:** Slow to moderate, paced for words, natural swing, or a communal singalong.
- **Groove:** Human and lightly syncopated; brushed percussion, foot-stomp, handclap, or unadorned strum can carry it.
- **Core instruments:** Acoustic guitar, banjo, mandolin, fiddle, upright bass, sparse hand percussion.
- **Vocal delivery:** Close, conversational, and story-led; harmonies feel communal rather than glossy.
- **Arrangement movement:** Begin spare, add instruments as the story expands, then return to a human-scale ending.
- **Mix character:** Warm, woody, and intimate, with audible fingers, breath, room, and string texture.
- **Compatible fusions:** Pop, rock, cinematic, Chinese-inspired fusion, restrained electronic ambience.
- **Likely conflicts:** Industrial percussion, hyper-polished pop vocals, or maximal metal density can erase folk's directness.

## R&B/soul

- **Tempo tendency:** Slow to mid-tempo, often with relaxed pocket and room for vocal phrasing.
- **Groove:** Deep bass, swung or behind-the-beat drums, syncopated chords, and pocket over speed.
- **Core instruments:** Electric piano, warm bass, drums, guitar fills, organ, pads, and expressive background vocals.
- **Vocal delivery:** Nuanced, melismatic where appropriate, emotionally detailed, with call-and-response possibilities.
- **Arrangement movement:** Build through vocal layering, chord color, and bass/drum pocket rather than abrupt density jumps.
- **Mix character:** Warm low mids, smooth high end, intimate lead vocal, and a rounded rhythmic foundation.
- **Compatible fusions:** Pop, hip-hop, jazz, electronic, cinematic strings in moderation.
- **Likely conflicts:** Rigid industrial attack, fast thrash-metal aggression, or overly sparse folk arrangements may undercut the pocket.

## Hip-hop

- **Tempo tendency:** Slow to moderate or double-time, defined by beat feel rather than raw BPM alone.
- **Groove:** Kick, snare/clap, hi-hat pattern, sub bass, and rhythmic space for flow.
- **Core instruments:** Drum machine, 808/sub bass, sampled or synthesized motifs, keys, chopped vocal textures.
- **Vocal delivery:** Rhythmic, articulate rap or spoken cadence; sung hooks can provide contrast.
- **Arrangement movement:** Beat drops and subtractions frame verses; hooks introduce a memorable tonal or vocal center.
- **Mix character:** Focused low end, crisp percussion, forward vocal intelligibility, and uncluttered center space.
- **Compatible fusions:** R&B/soul, pop, electronic, jazz samples, cinematic atmosphere.
- **Likely conflicts:** Long, unchanging orchestral density or busy guitar arrangements can crowd cadence and bass impact.

## Electronic

- **Tempo tendency:** Broad, from slow ambient pulse to fast club tempo; choose BPM to match the intended motion.
- **Groove:** Programmed repetition, sequenced rhythm, sidechain motion, and deliberate tension/release.
- **Core instruments:** Synth bass, pads, arpeggiators, drum machines, sampled textures, and processed transitions.
- **Vocal delivery:** Can be airy, chopped, heavily processed, or clean and pop-forward depending on the substyle.
- **Arrangement movement:** Add/remove layers, automate timbre, build risers, create drops, and vary rhythmic density.
- **Mix character:** Controlled sub, wide stereo image, sculpted frequency bands, and intentional spatial effects.
- **Compatible fusions:** Pop, hip-hop, R&B/soul, cinematic, rock texture.
- **Likely conflicts:** Unprocessed acoustic intimacy or loose jazz interplay can feel pasted on unless electronic motion stays subordinate.

## Jazz

- **Tempo tendency:** Flexible: ballad, medium swing, up-tempo swing, or modern straight-eighth groove.
- **Groove:** Swing, syncopation, pocket interaction, and space for rhythmic conversation.
- **Core instruments:** Piano, upright or electric bass, drum kit, saxophone, trumpet, guitar, and occasional strings.
- **Vocal delivery:** Phrasing-led, expressive, and capable of conversational timing rather than strict grid alignment.
- **Arrangement movement:** Head/theme, improvisatory variation, traded lines or solos, then a purposeful return.
- **Mix character:** Natural dynamics, instrument separation, realistic room, and enough warmth to preserve harmonic detail.
- **Compatible fusions:** R&B/soul, hip-hop, folk, cinematic, restrained electronic texture.
- **Likely conflicts:** Hyper-quantized EDM drops, rigid pop symmetry, or dense metal distortion can suppress harmonic and rhythmic nuance.

## Metal

- **Tempo tendency:** Often fast or forceful mid-tempo, with double-time and halftime changes for impact.
- **Groove:** Heavy riff lock, double-kick drive, palm-muted chugs, odd-meter or breakdown weight depending on the substyle.
- **Core instruments:** Distorted electric guitars, electric bass, large drum kit, occasional orchestral or electronic layers.
- **Vocal delivery:** Ranges from powerful clean belts to screams, growls, and gang shouts; select one clear lead approach.
- **Arrangement movement:** Riff changes, tension-building pre-choruses, breakdown/bridge contrast, and a larger final return.
- **Mix character:** Dense but separated guitars, forceful low end, aggressive upper mids, and controlled cymbal energy.
- **Compatible fusions:** Rock, cinematic, electronic, folk accents, hip-hop rhythm in selected substyles.
- **Likely conflicts:** Soft lullaby intimacy, fragile acoustic lead, or smooth jazz harmony becomes incoherent unless metal is explicitly reduced to an accent.

## Cinematic

- **Tempo tendency:** Flexible and scene-led; pulse may be subtle at first and accelerate or expand toward a cue point.
- **Groove:** Percussive ostinato, swells, taiko-like impacts, or sparse ambient pulse rather than a constant song beat.
- **Core instruments:** Strings, brass, piano, orchestral percussion, choir, sound design, and hybrid synth layers.
- **Vocal delivery:** Optional; if present, often textural, soaring, choral, or intimate against a spacious backdrop.
- **Arrangement movement:** Establish atmosphere, introduce motif, accumulate register and percussion, then resolve or suspend after a climax.
- **Mix character:** Wide, deep, dramatic, and dynamic, with room for transient impacts and orchestral tail.
- **Compatible fusions:** Pop, rock, electronic, folk, metal, Chinese-inspired fusion.
- **Likely conflicts:** Deliberately tiny lo-fi rooms, nonstop rap-density, or unvaried dance loops can prevent cinematic scale.

## Chinese-inspired fusion

- **Tempo tendency:** Variable; select a contemporary beat or ballad pace, then let the melodic ornament serve the phrase.
- **Groove:** Modern pop, hip-hop, electronic, or folk pulse combined with restrained traditional rhythmic color.
- **Core instruments:** Guzheng, pipa, erhu, dizi/xiao, Chinese percussion, pentatonic or modal motifs alongside modern rhythm section.
- **Vocal delivery:** Mandarin-led or bilingual; clear diction, natural tones, and controlled ornament rather than caricature.
- **Arrangement movement:** Introduce a recognizable traditional timbre or motif, contrast it with modern sections, then recombine both at the climax.
- **Mix character:** Preserve acoustic articulation and space around traditional instruments; avoid burying them under bright synths or dense guitars.
- **Compatible fusions:** Folk, pop, cinematic, electronic, hip-hop, rock in measured amounts.
- **Likely conflicts:** Generic “Asian” sound effects, unrelated pentatonic clichés, or maximal distortion that removes instrumental identity.

## Conflict resolution and priority

When two descriptors conflict, name the **lead** trait first and make the other an **accent**. The lead controls tempo behavior, groove, vocal distance, arrangement architecture, and mix priority; the accent contributes a limited timbre, rhythmic gesture, harmonic color, or transition. Do not ask two incompatible traits to be the lead at the same time.

| Conflict | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Acoustic + industrial | **Acoustic leads; industrial is an accent.** Keep acoustic guitar, close room, and human phrasing central; add restrained metallic hits, filtered machine percussion, or a brief processed transition. |
| Intimate vocal + stadium anthem | **Intimate vocal leads; stadium anthem is an accent.** Keep verses and the primary vocal close and detailed; reserve gang harmonies, wide drums, and a brief chorus lift for the anthem accent. |
| Lo-fi + cinematic orchestral | **Lo-fi leads; cinematic orchestral is an accent.** Retain the small, soft, degraded beat and narrow-room feeling; use a sparse string motif, quiet brass swell, or one distant climax rather than a full orchestral wall. |
| Aggressive metal + soft lullaby | **Soft lullaby leads; aggressive metal is an accent.** Use gentle tempo, soothing vocal, and simple harmony; add only low, distant guitar texture, a muted tom swell, or a brief heavy shadow without breaking the lullaby's safety. |

Before finalizing a prompt, state the genre priority in one sentence: “Lead with [genre/trait]; use [conflicting trait] as a restrained accent.” This turns conflict resolution into an actionable arrangement and mix decision.

## Version A and Version B differentiation

For two versions of one song, Version B must use a different structure or a different **lead** genre/arrangement axis from Version A; its accent alone may not carry the difference. Change a consequential choice such as groove, vocal distance, arrangement movement, or mix character with the new lead. Cosmetic word changes, an added ear-candy sound, or swapping one accent instrument do not qualify. Write the lead and the changed axis in both prompts so the distinction is testable.
