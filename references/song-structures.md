# Song Structures

Choose the structure from the song's use case before generating lyrics or arranging the production. A structure is a map for contrast and payoff, not a reason to repeat a section after its job is done.

## Short video

**Sequence:** Hook → Verse → Chorus → Tag

Use this for a short-form video, teaser, or brief cue where recognition has to arrive immediately. Put the title phrase, signature melodic cell, or most distinctive production gesture in the Hook; the Tag is a compact final repetition or answer that makes the clip loop cleanly.

## Standard single

**Sequence:** Intro → Verse 1 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → Final Chorus → Outro

Use this as the default for a full contemporary vocal song that needs setup, two verse perspectives, and a clear final lift. The Pre-Chorus increases expectation, the Bridge changes lyrical or harmonic perspective, and the Final Chorus earns a larger delivery than the first two.

## Narrative

**Sequence:** Verse 1 → Verse 2 → Chorus → Verse 3 → Bridge → Final Chorus

Use this when the lyric needs multiple chronological scenes, a developing point of view, or a consequential reveal. The delayed first chorus lets the listener enter the story before the main claim; Verse 3 should move the plot rather than restate the first two verses.

## Rock escalation

**Sequence:** Intro → Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Breakdown/Bridge → Final Chorus

Use this for band-forward energy that should intensify through guitar weight, drum density, vocal force, or a final key/riff expansion. Let the Breakdown/Bridge reduce or redirect the momentum briefly so the Final Chorus can return with the greatest impact.

## Instrumental

**Sequence:** Intro → Theme A → Theme B → Development → Climax → Outro

Use this for score-like music, beat-led tracks, or instrumental songs without a lyric-driven chorus. Theme A establishes the recognizable motif; Theme B supplies contrast; Development transforms one or both ideas; Climax gives the largest register, density, or rhythmic release.

## Labels and duration

Use labels when they help the generator or collaborators distinguish functions, especially for lyrics: `[Verse 1]`, `[Pre-Chorus]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`, and `[Outro]`. Labels may be omitted when the prompt itself gives an unambiguous instrumental roadmap, when the music is through-composed, or when a short-form prompt needs only a concise sequence. Omitting a label does not omit its musical function: describe the transition in the arrangement notes instead.

Duration changes section count and repetition, not the need for a beginning, contrast, and ending. For roughly 15–45 seconds, use the Short video structure or remove a repeated verse/chorus from a longer template. For about 2–3 minutes, use one verse cycle and a concise bridge or instrumental development. For about 3–4 minutes, use the full Standard single, Narrative, or Rock escalation sequence. Beyond that, add an instrumental turnaround, a new narrative scene, or a purposeful variation of a returning chorus; do not add duplicate sections solely to fill time.

## Version A and Version B differentiation

When presenting two versions of the same song, define Version A's structure and lead arrangement first. Version B must make one meaningful change: select a different structure template, or change the lead genre/arrangement axis (for example, acoustic folk story-song to electronic pop with drop-led movement). Changing only descriptive words, a minor instrument, or an accent is cosmetic and does not make a distinct Version B. State the changed axis explicitly in each version prompt.
